1. Scope of This Document
This document covers only the Accounts module Sales Department section of the SPD Concrete application. It
records the current UI workflow observed in the application, the missing controls/loopholes identified, the approved
fixes decided in discussion, the control words/status labels to be used in the application, a practical legal-validity
assessment for Indian GST and accounting workflow purposes, and the automation potential of the workflow.
Important note: This is a workflow and compliance design note, not a substitute for advice from the company
CA, GST practitioner, or legal advisor. The software can make the process safer, but final legal validity
depends on correct statutory invoicing, e-invoicing, e-way billing, tax returns, book entries, and audit
practices.

3.2 Final Improved Sales Reimbursement Workflow
1. Sales agent submits reimbursement claim based on verified workdays.
2. Manager verifies or corrects the claim amount and forwards it to Accounts.
3. Accounts selects payment type: full, partial, hold, or reject.
4. For cash payment, Accounts must generate a cash voucher before OTP is sent.
5. Cash voucher must record voucher number, claim ID, agent, paid amount, cash paid by accountant, payment
date/time, accountant employee ID, and remarks.
6. After cash voucher creation, OTP is sent to the agent for receipt confirmation.
7. After OTP verification, the claim is marked paid or partially paid based on actual amount paid.
8. If partial payment is made, the balance remains in outstanding ledger.

9. Legal-Validity Assessment
The approved workflow is legally sensible as an internal control system, but it is not automatically legally complete
merely because the app records approvals. Legal validity depends on whether the final documents and statutory
filings meet applicable GST/accounting rules.
9.1 Cash Reimbursement and Cash Voucher Validity
Creating a cash voucher for every reimbursement is a strong and commonly accepted accounting control. It should
include voucher number, date, paid-to person, amount in figures and words, purpose, claim reference, accountant
name/ID, receiver confirmation, and remarks. For stronger audit quality, the system should also keep a printable
voucher and immutable audit log.
9.2 GST Client and Odoo Ledger Validity
Using Odoo ledger for GST clients is appropriate because GST client billing needs clean statutory invoice, GSTIN,
billing address, legal name, tax break-up, HSN, place of supply, and invoice numbering control. The official CGST
Rule 46 tax invoice requirements include supplier GSTIN, unique serial number, date, recipient details, HSN,
description, quantity, value, tax rate, tax amount, place of supply, delivery address where applicable, reverse-charge
indication, signature/digital signature, and QR/IRN where applicable.
9.3 Non-GST Client and Internal Ledger Validity
Using an internal ledger for non-GST clients is acceptable for internal receivable tracking. However, if SPD Concrete
is a GST registered supplier making taxable outward supply, non-GST/unregistered customer billing may still require
a GST-compliant tax invoice or bill of supply/challan as applicable. Therefore, a non-GST client does not mean no
statutory document is required; it only means the customer does not have GSTIN and may not require Odoo customer
ledger posting in the same way as GST clients.
9.4 E-Invoice and E-Way Bill Caution
If SPD Concrete falls under e-invoicing applicability, B2B GST invoices must be reported through the Invoice
Registration Portal/approved provider before being treated as proper e-invoice documents. For goods movement, e-
way bill rules generally require information before movement for applicable consignments and the person in charge
of the conveyance must carry invoice/bill of supply/delivery challan and e-way bill or e-way bill number where
required.

12. Automation Potential and Human Intervention Requirement
Because this application is mainly focused on automation, the Accounts Sales Department workflow should be
divided into three categories: fully automated, semi-automated, and human-approval-required. The target should not
be 100% automation of finance decisions. The safer design is to automate clean, rule-based checks and push only
risky or exception cases to Accounts, Manager, or higher approval.
Overall automation assessment: Around 70-80% of the Accounts Sales workflow can be automated safely without
direct human intervention. The remaining 20-30% should stay under human control because it involves money, tax,
legal billing risk, credit exposure, or disputed documents.

12.2 Parts That Can Be Fully Automated
The following activities are rule-based and can be automated with very low risk if validation rules are correctly
implemented:
 Checking whether mandatory fields are present: customer, site, billing address, grade, quantity, rate, receiver,
required date/time, plant, and payment type.
 Checking GSTIN structure, PAN extraction from GSTIN, state code, and whether GST fields are complete.
 Checking whether PO, PDC, GST certificate, and payment proof are uploaded when required.
 Checking duplicate UTR/reference number, duplicate request, and duplicate document upload.
 Checking whether the customer has an existing ledger by GSTIN, phone number, PAN, name, billing address, or
site address.
 Checking whether order quantity and rate match approved final approval terms.
 Checking whether mix design is available and approved for selected grade and plant.

12.3 Parts That Should Be Semi-Automated
The following parts should be AI/rule-assisted, but the final decision should be made by Accounts or Manager when
the system detects uncertainty:
 GST legal name and billing address matching, especially when certificate, GST API, and customer-entered data
do not perfectly match.
 PO/PDC content verification where OCR can read customer name, PO number, quantity, grade, site,
signature/stamp, and date, but unclear or mismatched documents require human review.
 Advance payment verification where the app can check amount, UTR, date, and proof, but bank/API
confirmation may still be required.
 Ledger creation for customers with similar names, multiple sites, missing GST, or same phone number across
different customers.
 Credit sale checks where the system can show outstanding, overdue, credit limit, and risk category, but over-limit
approval should be manual.
 Reimbursement cases involving abnormal distance, late submission, OCR mismatch, duplicate image, or edited
GPS/date watermark.
 Customer payment credit entry without bank integration, especially cash, cheque, or third-party payment cases.
12.4 Parts That Should Not Be Fully Automated
The following should remain human-approved because they involve financial, legal, credit, or audit risk:
 Final approval for over-credit-limit or overdue customer orders.
 Approval of missing PO/PDC document exceptions.
 Approval of GST mismatch, billing-name mismatch, or suspicious GST certificate cases.
 Approval of high-value sales order release to production.
 Approval of non-GST order edits, cancelled-order reuse, or repeated manual modifications.
 Confirmation of actual cash reimbursement payment release.
 Approval of partial payment, hold payment, or rejected reimbursement claim.
 Any case where payment proof is unclear, UTR is missing, cheque is not realized, or payment amount does not
match required amount.
 Director/management approval for blocked/high-risk customers or unusual commercial exceptions.
12.5 Recommended Automation Design: Auto-Process Clean Cases, Escalate Exceptions
The best workflow is not to send every request manually to Accounts. Instead, the app should automatically process
clean cases and escalate only exception cases.

12.7 Final Automation Conclusion
The Accounts Sales Department can safely automate approximately 70-80% of the workflow. The application should
automate data checking, GST format checking, document presence checking, duplicate detection, ledger suggestions,
clean sales order preparation, pipeline tracking, customer ledger debit posting, and reimbursement calculation.
However, the system should not fully automate risky finance decisions. The remaining 20-30% must stay under
human control, especially credit approval, payment confirmation, GST mismatch, duplicate ledger decision, high-
value orders, cash reimbursement payments, non-GST order edits/cancellations, and any case where money, tax, or
legal billing risk is involved.
